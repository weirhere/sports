## Decision

**A series game is its round, and the conference round has one name per league.** `TrophyKind.from(headline:league:)` and `trophyKindFromHeadline` now drop a trailing "- Game N" (hyphen or en dash) before matching anything. They also read the NBA's and NHL's conference round whole, on its side alone: "Eastern Conference Finals", "East Finals" and the NHL's "East Final" are one trophy, named "Eastern Conference Finals" in the NBA and "Eastern Conference Final" in the NHL. Before this, no NHL team derived a Stanley Cup. Each conference-final game was also its own trophy, so the Celtics were credited with a 2017 East title for winning Game 3 of a series they lost 4–1, and 2024's "East Finals" matched nothing.

## Why

This came out of COR-25's first live run of the web trophy endpoint. That run also showed the multi-season fetch the issue asked for had already shipped in #148. Then I probed ESPN's NBA and NHL postseason headlines for 2014–26 across 18 teams. Every series game ends in "- Game N", which failed `decidesATrophy`'s "ends in final" test. It also split the series-is-decided-by-its-last-game rule into one key per game. The conference round has had three spellings in that span. Matching the round whole, rather than as a substring, also keeps "East Semifinals" and "Eastern Conference First Round" out. The NHL keeps "Final" because that is the league's own name and what the existing tests already expected. After the change, the endpoint gives the Panthers two Cups, Vegas and Colorado one each, and the Celtics their 2022 and 2024 East titles without the 2017 one.
