## Decision

**The NFL's league titles go all-time, on two rows.** `TrophyRegistry` gets the NFL's two verified lists (in `TrophyRegistry+NFL.swift`). `nflChampionships` is a new league-tier kind, "NFL Championship", covering 1920–1965: 40 seasons, with no row for the six champions that have no successor team (1920 Akron, 1922–23 Canton, 1924 Cleveland Bulldogs, 1926 Frankford, 1928 Providence). `superBowls` covers the 1966–2013 seasons, Super Bowls I–XLVIII, and the wire still derives 2014 on. Titles are filed by franchise. The Baltimore Colts' titles are Indianapolis's, the Raiders' are Las Vegas's, the Cleveland and St. Louis Rams' are Los Angeles's, and the Browns' stay in Cleveland. Neither list carries a `source`. The Packers' shelf reads NFL Championships 9 above Super Bowls 4, and the Steelers' reads Super Bowls 6.

## Why

This applies COR-94's second and third calls (Andy, 2026-11-04). The cut at 1965 means no season holds both an NFL Championship and a Super Bowl, since the 1966–69 NFL title games fed one. The standings-decided seasons before 1933 count because the NFL counts them, and the AFL's titles stay out. Neither list needs a caption the way college football's does: no selector choice changes any current team's count, so the trophy is its own source. The rows were checked season by season against Wikipedia's "List of NFL champions (1920–1969)" and "List of Super Bowl champions". Every ESPN id is resolved in the tests from an abbreviation in a captured `nfl/teams` payload, so a mistyped id fails as a wrong total. The per-franchise totals (10 franchises summing to 40, 19 summing to 48) were counted from the source lists, not from the rows. Within the league tier the shelf's existing most-won-first order applies, so the Packers lead with NFL Championships. That's the existing rule, not a new one.
