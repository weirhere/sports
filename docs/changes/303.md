## Decision

**The game page's Standings tab reaches the web.** Between Roster and H2H, as on iOS: `web/src/lib/game-standings.ts` is `GameStandingsModel.tables(at:)`'s twin (tables found by membership, away's first, a shared table once), `/api/game/[gameId]/standings` serves the game's own season's tables on the tab's first open (each side's division in college football, the pros' conference and divisional responses together through `hubStandings`), and `StandingsList` takes `highlightTeamIds` and an `elevated` fill. The team page's chip opens on Conference.

## Why

Parity for #287. One route call answers every scope on web, because `hubStandings` already returns the conference and divisional responses side by side, so the scope chip costs no request where iOS's Division costs a second one. The two highlighted rows don't scroll into view: there's no single row to land on, and switching tabs mustn't move the page.

## Decision

**The WNBA's game Standings chip offers League and Conference, not Division.** `GameStandingsModel.scopes(in:)` offered all three scopes to any league with a league-wide table. The WNBA (added 2026-11-03) has conferences and no divisions, so its Division chip would have tabled nothing and said "Standings TBA". Scopes now follow `Conference.hasDivisions(in:)`, the team page's rule. The college-football division fetch also asks `league.hasCollegeDivisions` rather than `== .collegeFootball`.

## Why

#287 was built when the app had four leagues. College basketball, MLB and the WNBA landed after it. College basketball and MLB already read correctly; the WNBA was the one that didn't.
