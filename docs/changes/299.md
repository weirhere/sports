## Decision

**Men's and women's college basketball, then MLB and the WNBA, at full parity, iOS and web.** Two
new leagues, `mensCollegeBasketball` (`"mcbb"`, NCAAM) and
`womensCollegeBasketball` (`"wcbb"`, NCAAW): Scores, game pages (the NBA's
basketball detail, court Gamecast included), team pages, conference pages and
standings, the AP and Coaches polls, the NCAA tournament bracket, trophies,
follows, search and the widget. Shipped the night both seasons opened.

- **League gets a sport axis and a college axis.** `League.sport`
  (`isFootball`, `isBasketball`), `isCollege` and `divisionGroupId` (80 FBS,
  50 Division I, nil for the pros). The 66 `league == .collegeFootball` sites
  each now ask the question they meant: *college* (polls, class years,
  conference-first slate and headers, CONF/OVR standings, no trades), *football*
  (drives, formation, field), or *FBS/FCS* (`hasCollegeDivisions`, which stays
  football's alone). `hasPoll` is `isCollege`; the CFP is `hasPlayoffRanking`.
- **Division I, every game.** Scoreboard and standings ask `groups=50`
  (without it ESPN answers with a curated handful); 182 men's and 164 women's
  games on the opening Monday. The slate is Top 25 plus one section per
  conference, like NCAAF.
- **College basketball's conference sections start collapsed.** Top 25 and
  Following stay open. ~350 games a night, each in two conference sections,
  would put seven hundred rows between NCAAF and the NFL.
- **Men play halves, women quarters.** `periodFormat` 2 × HALF vs 4 ×
  QUARTER; the status line's ordinals make the men's third period "OT" for
  free. Late-game and closeness rules follow.
- **A hardcoded conference table**, shared by both leagues except the Summit
  League (49 men, 47 women), ESPN's "Metro" named the MAAC, and the five high
  majors (ACC, Big East, Big Ten, Big 12, SEC) leading. 31 of 32 marks under
  `ncaa_conf`; the UAC has none, as in football.
- **Follows stay per league.** Following Michigan football doesn't follow
  Michigan basketball.
- **The NCAA tournament is the bracket**, named off headlines (First Four
  through National Championship). The NIT, WBIT and Crown name nothing, like
  bowls. Trophies: the NCAA final and conference tournament finals.
- **An NBA player's College row opens the school's NCAAM page** (`League.collegeFeeder`);
  NFL and NHL keep football. NCAAM's Pros tab lists NBA players; NCAAW lists
  none until the app has the WNBA.
- **Polls read the end-year season** at the core API boundary, and the
  final basketball poll is the season's last listed week (postseason week 3),
  not football's week 1.

**MLB and the WNBA (2026-11-03, the same PR).** `League.mlb` and `.wnba`, and `Sport.baseball`. The WNBA rides every basketball case. MLB gets the rest:

- **Calendar-year seasons.** `League.seasonSpansNewYear` is false for both. Such a league closes its season in the year it opened and shows last season until its opener. The day strip's July–June season is computed over the cross-year leagues only, so September stays in the current season. `hasWeeks` and `playsOnASurface` read the sport instead of the season shape.
- **No clock.** A live MLB line is ESPN's own "Top 5th". Extra innings are the 10th, never OT.
- **Registries.** MLB has AL/NL over six divisions, with all 30 teams placed; "AL East" doesn't grow a "(American League)". The WNBA has two conferences, 15 teams and no divisions, so its slate offers no Division rung. Id 1 is now four different groups across leagues, tested.
- **Standings.** MLB pins and scrolls (W L PCT GB HOME AWAY RS RA DIFF STRK L10). For a calendar-year league, a table from an earlier season than the one ESPN announces keeps its numbers: all winter ESPN announces 2027 over 2026's final, and MLB's current season is still 2026. Cross-year leagues keep the preseason blanking.
- **The game page.** Half-innings; the Plays tab lists at-bat results under "TOP 1ST"-style headings (the feed is ~516 pitches a game); R H E; Batting/Pitching box score; grouped team stats in the compare card; a Scoring card off the flat feed.
- **The live diamond.** `BaseballDiamond` reads runners (bases after the play), outs (the latest finished at-bat's), the count, the matchup and the last pitch off the play feed. The card is Count · Outs · Pitch over a `DiamondSurface`. The color budget's surface exception widens to the diamond: the turf's own green, a new infield dirt, and occupied bags in the batting team's color.
- **Reminders.** MLB teams start with no kickoff reminder; the bell turns it on.
- **Also:** World Series, pennants, WNBA Finals and Commissioner's Cup trophies; series-based playoffs get no bracket, like the NBA; popular teams; positions; a diamond formation on the Roster tab.

## Why

Both seasons tipped off 2026-11-02 and Andy wanted them in "as soon as
possible". The icebox row said it needed no new machinery, and the probe
agreed: every summary, standings and rankings payload is the NBA's or
NCAAF's shape. The real work was the 66 gates that had used "college
football" as a synonym for three different things. Splitting them by meaning
is what keeps the next college sport (or the WNBA) from repeating this
audit.

MLB and the WNBA were E17, scoped since September with its scope decision left open. Andy closed it the day after college basketball: both leagues, MLB with its own live shape, in the same PR. The play feed made the live shape cheap: every MLB play carries outs, the count, the runners and the matchup, so the diamond reads like the court's shot map, off the latest plays, and doesn't depend on a scoreboard `situation` that can't be probed until spring. Reminders are off by default because six games a week would crowd every other follow out of the nearest-24 window. The calendar-year season rule was the one change that touched every league, so it landed first, with the existing leagues pinned to the day.

## Web parity

shipped — for MLB and the WNBA too: both leagues, their registries, the calendar-year season rule, the clock-aware status line, R/H/E, half-inning plays, the diamond card and the winter-standings rule. Reminders are iOS-only (n/a) and the web's compare card is football-only for every league (differs). For college basketball: `web/src/lib/leagues.ts` gains both leagues and the same axes
(`isCollege`, `sportOf`, `leagueGroupId`, `collegeFeeder`); the poll page takes
`?league=`; college basketball's conference sections start closed via a new
`openedSections` list; tests in `college-basketball.test.ts`.
