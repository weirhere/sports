### The Siri half — a question, not a task

iOS 27's Siri acts on apps **through App Intents**, and **SiriKit is retired**. StatSide has never used SiriKit, so there is nothing to migrate and no breakage to fix — the exposure is the opposite one: *absence*. The app's entire assistant surface is **one intent**, `Intents/NextGameIntent.swift` ("What's my next game?") plus a two-phrase `AppShortcutsProvider`. No `AppEntity`, no `IndexedEntity`, nothing contributed to the Spotlight semantic index. An assistant that can now reference a user's content can reference none of ours: not a team, not a game, not a score.

- [ ] **P1 — decide before building.** Whether StatSide models teams and games as `AppEntity`/`IndexedEntity` so Siri and Spotlight can name them, and whether the intent surface grows past the single "next game" question (a score for a team, today's slate, a followed team's result). This is a real feature with a real surface, so per CLAUDE.md it gets discussed before it gets coded. Two things to establish **against the shipped SDK rather than from launch coverage**, because both change the shape of the work: (1) whether any **App Schema** covers a sports/scores domain at all — the schema list in circulation (mail, photos, books, journal, presentations, spreadsheets, system) suggests **not**, which would mean custom intents and entities rather than conforming to a typed contract; (2) whether App Intents 2.0's streaming and multi-turn responses need a deployment target above 18.0 to use, which would put them behind `#available` alongside the glass.

  The product argument for doing it: the app's whole thesis is time-to-score, and "hey Siri, what's the Michigan score" is the shortest path to a score there has ever been — it beats opening the app, which is the thing we optimize. The argument for waiting: Siri AI is English-only at rollout and the feature is worth nothing until the entity index is actually populated and tested on a device.
